Insights

What is urban planning?

Urban planning decides how land in cities and regions is used, connected and looked after, so that people can live, work and move well.

What urban planners decide

Planners set where homes, jobs, schools, parks and services go, how streets and public transport connect them, how dense an area can be, and how fast and in what order it can grow. They also look at water, energy, climate and heritage, because a city works only if these systems work together.

How a plan is made

Most plans follow the same path: understand the place and its people, set a vision, test scenarios, choose a preferred structure, define rules and phasing, and agree how it will be delivered and paid for. Good plans are tested with the people who live there before they are fixed.

Why it matters

Choices drawn on paper become streets, schools and homes that last for generations. A well-planned city is easier to move through, safer, more affordable to serve and more pleasant to live in. This is why PUD describes its work as helping people live a better life.

Related question

Who needs an urban planner?

Governments, municipalities, developers, universities and investors all use urban planners when they build a new city, extend an existing one, redevelop a district or prepare a regional strategy.
